K5 Bearing

Body · Movement · Iron · Prayer

The body is the instrument — of work and of worship. These are notes on keeping it: how we move it, build it, and bow it. Effort and reverence run through the same flesh. The whole aim is simple. Keep your bearing.

I The Body

The body is the first thing given to us and the last thing we set down. Everything begins here. To know yourself, begin with the body — its hunger, its limits, its strange willingness to be built. It is not an obstacle to the spirit but the instrument of it.

Care for it plainly. Feed it, rest it, use it. A body attended to becomes quiet enough to think in; a body neglected grows loud and makes every other thing harder.

II Movement

We were made to move. Kept still, the body dims; set in motion, it clears the head. Walk, carry, climb, work with the hands. None of it has to be grand. Movement is the daily proof that you are awake and in the world.

Let some of it be honest and unmeasured — a long walk, a hard day's labour — the kind of motion that asks nothing of a screen.

III The Iron

The gym is a school of the will. Under the bar there is no arguing with the weight — you move it or you do not. Strength is earned in small, repeated acts, and it teaches what little else can: that you become what you practise.

Show up. Add a little. Return tomorrow. The iron keeps an honest ledger and pays out slowly, which is exactly why it is worth trusting.

IV Prayer

Karaite prayer returns to the source — the Tanakh, the written word, read and spoken plainly before God. It is worship kept close to Scripture: the Psalms on the lips, the knees bent, the face lowered to the ground.

Here the body and the spirit meet. The same flesh that strains under the bar bows in prostration; the same breath that counts a lift carries a psalm. To pray this way is to bring the whole body into reverence — nothing held back, nothing left standing.

Keep your bearing. Kastle Five Systems
