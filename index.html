K5 Bearing

Body · Physical Training · Prayer

The body is the instrument — of work and of worship. These are notes on keeping it: how we move it, and how we bow it. Effort and reverence run through the same flesh. The whole aim is simple. Keep your bearing.

I The Body

The body is the first thing given to us and the last thing we set down. Everything begins here. To know yourself, begin with the body — its hunger, its limits, its strange willingness to be built. It is not an obstacle to the spirit but the instrument of it.

Care for it plainly. Feed it, rest it, use it. A body attended to becomes quiet enough to think in; a body neglected grows loud and makes every other thing harder.

II Physical Training

We were made to move. Kept still, the body dims; set in motion, it clears the head. Walk, carry, climb, work with the hands. None of it has to be grand. Motion is the daily proof that you are awake and in the world.

Let some of it be honest and unmeasured — a long walk, a hard day's labour — the kind of motion that asks nothing of a screen.

One barbell, one end anchored in a floor pivot: the landmine. It arcs instead of travelling straight, which is kinder to the shoulders and steadier under load. Nearly everything the body needs can be trained on it.

  • Press — one arm at a time, driving the sleeve up and away. Overhead strength without the overhead strain.
  • Shoulder-to-shoulder press — both hands on the end, pressing it across from one shoulder to the other.
  • Thruster — a front squat driven straight up into a press. The whole body in one movement.
  • Front squat — the end held at the chest; the arc keeps you upright and honest.
  • Hack squat — the bar behind you, the load carried by the legs.
  • Reverse lunge — loaded at the chest, one leg stepping back at a time.
  • Romanian deadlift — hinge at the hips, hamstrings long and loaded.
  • Single-leg deadlift — the same hinge on one leg. Balance is the point.
  • Meadows row — staggered stance, one arm rowing the sleeve to the hip.
  • T-bar row — both hands, chest over the bar, pulled to the ribs.
  • Rotation — the bar swung in an arc across the body. Trunk, obliques, control.
  • Deadlift — straddle the bar, take the end in both hands, and stand up with it.

Start light and learn the arc. Add a little, return tomorrow.

III Prayer

Karaite prayer returns to the source — the Tanakh, the written word, read and spoken plainly before God. It is worship kept close to Scripture: the Psalms on the lips, the knees bent, the face lowered to the ground.

Here the body and the spirit meet. The same flesh that strains under a load bows in prostration; the same breath that counts an effort carries a psalm. To pray this way is to bring the whole body into reverence — nothing held back, nothing left standing.

Keep your bearing. — Kastle Five Systems
